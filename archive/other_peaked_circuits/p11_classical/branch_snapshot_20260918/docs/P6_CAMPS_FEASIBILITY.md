# CAMPS feasibility for P5/P6/P8/P9 — closed

**Verdict: CAMPS offers no advantage on any circuit in this family, including the
three that were solved by other means.** Closed on a 20-minute diagnostic rather
than a multi-day build.

## Why it looked promising

All 3,494 P6 entanglers are CZ, hence Clifford. A Clifford-augmented MPS writes
the state as `C |phi>`: Clifford gates are absorbed into `C` for free, and a
non-Clifford rotation becomes

```
R_P(theta) C |phi>  =  C R_{P'}(theta) |phi>,     P' = C^dag P C
```

so only the *conjugated* rotations ever touch the MPS. If P6's error accrued
only on a small residual instead of compounding over all 2,593 gates, that would
address the route-decorrelation failure mode directly (see
`P6_MPO_DIAGNOSIS.md` section 9 and `P5_P6_P8_CAMPAIGN_REPORT.md` section 5).

## The diagnostic

`scripts/camps_nullity.py`. Tracks the Clifford frame as a symplectic tableau
(images of `C^dag X_j C` and `C^dag Z_j C`), conjugates each non-Clifford Pauli
rotation through it, and maintains an incremental GF(2) row-echelon basis per
MPS bond cut.

Across cut `k`, the bond is bounded by `2^nu_k`, where `nu_k` is the GF(2) rank
of the conjugated Pauli strings that straddle the cut, restricted to the
**smaller** side and capped at `2*min(|left|,|right|)`. Rotations that do not
straddle a cut cost nothing there, and GF(2)-dependent ones do not compound.

`U3(theta,phi,lam)` is decomposed as `Rz(phi) Ry(theta) Rz(lam)`; any rotation
whose angle is a multiple of pi/2 is absorbed into the frame. `iSWAP` is Clifford
(`SWAP . CZ . (S⊗S)`); `rzz(theta)` is Clifford only at multiples of pi/2 and
otherwise contributes the two-qubit Pauli `Z_a Z_b`.

Nine unit checks pass, covering frame conjugation (`H`, `S`, `CZ`, `iSWAP`),
the `H.CZ.H` case that produces a genuinely spanning `Z_0 Z_1`, and GF(2)
dependence.

**Bug found and fixed during development:** restricting the rank to the left side
always inflates edge cuts (61 qubits left, 1 right) into meaninglessly large
ranks -- the first run reported max nu of 80-113. The Schmidt rank across a cut
is bounded by the smaller subsystem.

## Result

| circuit | qubits | Clifford frac | non-Clifford rotations | max nu | bond bound | solved? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|:--:|
| P5 | 44 | 33.0% | 7,700 | **44** | 2^44 | **yes** |
| P8 | 40 | 14.6% | 5,408 | **40** | 2^40 | **yes** |
| P9 | 56 | 15.3% | 11,452 | **56** | 2^56 | **yes** |
| P6 | 62 | 36.7% | 14,308 | **62** | 2^62 | no |

**Every circuit saturates its maximum possible rank** -- nu equals
`min(|left|,|right|)` at the middle cut in all four cases. The Clifford-conjugated
Pauli rotations span the full symplectic space.

## Why the control matters

P5, P8 and P9 were all recovered by the MPO route, and all three saturate
identically. So this is not "P6 is too hard for CAMPS" -- it is **"CAMPS does not
apply to this circuit family."** A method that offers no advantage on circuits we
already solved cannot be expected to rescue the one we have not.

## Why the premise failed

P6 has the *highest* Clifford fraction of the four (36.7%) and still saturates.
"All entanglers are CZ, so the Clifford frame carries the entanglement for free"
is true but irrelevant: the entanglement that matters is generated by the
**single-qubit rotations**, and roughly two-thirds of those are non-Clifford --
14,308 of them after frame absorption. There is no small residual for a Clifford
frame to isolate.

## Caveats

- The bound is an upper bound. A smarter disentangling variant could in principle
  beat `2^nu`, but saturation across all four circuits indicates there is no
  structure left to exploit.
- `nu` is computed in the natural qubit ordering. A better ordering could lower
  it, though saturation at the middle cut of a 34.8%-dense interaction graph
  leaves little headroom.
